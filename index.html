📋Plan Overview

This plan is designed to intentionally move the group from initial connection to shared standards, trusted leadership, high performance, and a meaningful season-end transition. Team building will not be treated as a single social event. It will be an ongoing process built into meetings, practices, games, leadership opportunities, service, and reflection.

🤝Team Building Philosophy

I define a team as a group of people who understand why they are together, trust one another, accept clear roles, hold shared standards, and work consistently toward a purpose that is larger than any one person. My role as head coach is to create the conditions for belonging, accountability, learning, leadership, and performance. Players must have a voice in the culture, while the coaching staff remains responsible for making the environment safe, inclusive, consistent, and aligned with our program standards.

Purpose of This Plan

Build a connected team whose relationships and daily behaviours improve development, enjoyment, resilience, and competitive performance.

🧭Team Vision & Values

Team Vision

To become a connected, accountable, and resilient team that competes with purpose, develops every player, and represents our program with pride on the ice and in the community.

Daily mission: We prepare together, communicate honestly, support one another, and make team-first decisions.

Core Values and Observable Behaviours

Respect

We treat every person and place with dignity.

  • Listen when others speak.
  • Respect teammates, staff, officials, opponents, volunteers, and facilities.
  • Reject hazing, exclusion, and demeaning language.

Commitment

We follow through on what the team requires.

  • Arrive on time and prepared.
  • Compete with purpose in practice and games.
  • Take recovery, school/work, and communication seriously.

Accountability

We own our preparation, choices, and responses.

  • Use honest, solution-focused language.
  • Admit mistakes without blaming others.
  • Hold teammates to agreed standards respectfully.

Team First

We value every role and choose what helps the group.

  • Celebrate teammates' success.
  • Support role changes and next-player-up decisions.
  • Share information, effort, and responsibility.

Growth & Resilience

We use challenge, feedback, and mistakes to improve.

  • Ask for and apply feedback.
  • Reset quickly after adversity.
  • Stay curious, adaptable, and solution-oriented.

Team Promise

We will be prepared, honest, supportive, and courageous. We will compete for one another, learn from every experience, and leave our team and community better than we found them.

🔑Keys to Achieving Our Vision & Goals

1. Co-Ownership and Clarity

Players will help convert the vision and values into a short team charter. Expectations for preparation, communication, conduct, role acceptance, school/work responsibilities, and digital communication will be discussed rather than assumed.

Evidence: completed charter, player signatures/acknowledgement, and meeting notes.

2. Consistent Staff Modelling

Coaches and support staff must demonstrate the same punctuality, respect, emotional control, preparation, and accountability expected from players. Staff will use consistent language and address gaps privately, promptly, and fairly.

Evidence: staff alignment meetings and common communication standards.

3. Connection and Belonging

Every player should feel known, included, and valued. Early-season connection activities, rotating partners, small-group conversations, and regular check-ins will prevent cliques and help new or quieter players contribute.

Measure: culture-pulse questions on belonging and psychological safety.

4. Direct Communication and Healthy Conflict

Concerns will be addressed with the person involved, using respectful and specific language. The group will separate the behaviour from the person, listen before responding, and agree on a next action. Safety or maltreatment concerns will follow the appropriate reporting process immediately.

Measure: issues are addressed early, with a documented follow-up when required.

5. Role Clarity and Shared Leadership

Each player will understand how their role contributes to team success. A leadership group will support communication and standards, while other players receive rotating responsibilities such as warm-up leadership, meeting facilitation, community-project roles, and peer recognition.

Evidence: role meetings, leadership notes, and at least one player-led action each month.

6. Intentional Experiences and Service

Team building will include task-cohesion activities, social connection, problem-solving, gratitude, and community service. Every activity will have a stated purpose and a short debrief connecting the experience to team performance.

Evidence: calendar, attendance, debrief questions, and project outcomes.

7. Reinforcement, Reflection, and Adjustment

Values will be reinforced through specific recognition, not slogans alone. The team will complete monthly culture check-ins, a midseason reset, and a postseason review. The plan will be adjusted when team needs, schedule, injuries, or conflict require a different approach.

Measure: trend in survey results and documented changes made from feedback.

Operating Agreement

  • Be early, ready, and present.
  • Communicate absences or concerns through the agreed channel.
  • Challenge ideas without attacking people.
  • Keep private team matters private, except where safety requires reporting.
  • Recognize effort, improvement, and team-first behaviour.
Final wording will be co-created at the September values workshop.

Recurring Team-Building Habits

  • Weekly value recognition: identify one specific example of a player or unit living a team value.
  • Player-led preparation: rotating pairs lead a warm-up, recovery cue, or brief team message.
  • Monthly three-question culture pulse: belonging, role clarity, and confidence in team communication.
  • Post-game connection: one “keep,” one “learn,” and one “next action” before leaving the rink.
  • Leadership check-in: a short meeting every two weeks to surface concerns and plan player-led actions.

❤️Community Project, Charity & Fundraiser

Proposed Project: “Hockey Gives Back” Winter Essentials & Food Drive

The team will partner with [Local food bank, youth shelter, or community organization] to collect items identified by that organization as its highest-priority needs. With association and partner approval, the team may add a game-night donation station or sponsor-matched fundraiser. The project will be player-led, supervised by adults, and connected directly to the team values of respect, commitment, accountability, team first, and growth.

Project Objectives

  • Meet a verified community need rather than collecting items without partner direction.
  • Achieve full-team participation, with every player contributing through planning, promotion, collection, sorting, delivery, or reflection.
  • Set a measurable partner-approved donation or fundraising target before launch.
  • Complete one team delivery or volunteer experience when permitted by the partner and schedule.
  • Use a short debrief to connect service to gratitude, leadership, teamwork, and representing the program.

Success Measures

  • Percentage of players participating in an assigned role.
  • Total priority items, funds, or volunteer hours contributed.
  • Partner feedback on usefulness and organization.
  • Player reflection: one lesson about community, privilege, service, or leadership.
  • Completion of receipts, cash controls, permissions, and thank-you communication.

Roles and Responsibilities

Head Coach

Approves the learning purpose, protects practice/game priorities, confirms safe-sport expectations, and ensures the project reflects the team values.

Team Manager / Adult Lead

Coordinates the partner, facility, transportation, permissions, communication, and documentation. Oversees funds and receipts with another designated adult.

Player Leadership Group

Leads the player meeting, assigns roles, monitors participation, communicates the campaign, and facilitates the final reflection.

Players

Contribute in assigned roles such as promotion, collection, sorting, delivery, thank-you messages, and documenting lessons learned.

Parent / Volunteer Support

Provides approved transportation, staffing, and logistical support without taking ownership away from the players.

Community Partner

Identifies current needs, confirms safe procedures, receives the contribution, and provides feedback on the project's usefulness.

Safeguards and financial accountability: No player will handle cash alone. When fundraising is used, two designated adults will verify totals and records. A direct partner donation link will be preferred where available. All activities will follow team, association, facility, privacy, photo-consent, transportation, and safe-sport requirements.

📈Measurement, Review & Evidence

The quality of team building will be evaluated by changes in behaviour and connection, not only by attendance at activities. Data will be used to adjust the plan rather than to label individual players.

Culture Pulse

Anonymous 1–5 ratings in September, December, and March on belonging, trust, role clarity, communication, accountability, and confidence in the team direction.

Behavioural Observation

Staff track examples of values in action, communication under pressure, response to mistakes, bench support, punctuality, and role acceptance.

Player Leadership

Track player-led warm-ups, meetings, team messages, problem-solving decisions, peer recognition, and community-project responsibilities.

Role Clarity

Confirm that each player receives an initial role conversation and scheduled check-ins when roles or expectations change.

Conflict Response

Review whether concerns are surfaced early, handled directly and respectfully, followed up, and escalated immediately when safety or maltreatment is involved.

Community Impact

Record participation, items/funds/hours, partner feedback, player learning, and completion of required permissions and financial records.
